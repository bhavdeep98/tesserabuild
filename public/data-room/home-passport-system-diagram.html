Tessera · Executive architecture brief

One home identity. A continuous history. Authorized services.

Tessera connects builders, owners and service providers—preserving the home’s useful history while controlling who can see it and act on it.

Review notes · the safeguards behind this diagram

Authority: identity verification and approved ownership evidence are separate checks. Co-owners have independent bindings; transfer disputes and exceptional ownership require a reviewed recovery process. Owner-initiated services must not depend on the original builder’s continued involvement.

Enforcement: source read scopes and endpoint rules protect builder systems. Server-side policy and scoped database/service roles enforce access; drawing three boxes is not isolation. Transfer atomically changes the ownership version; new requests and queued jobs must check it. Privileged operations need independent security auditing.

Service safety: live grants limit recipient, purpose, fields and time. Sensitive fields are lent in a time-boxed view rather than transferred, so most never land in a provider's systems; only the minimum a provider genuinely needs (typically the address) transfers. Purchases need separate approval of terms. Use stable job identifiers and provider-supported duplicate prevention; reconcile ambiguous outcomes. Revocation blocks future access, but cannot retrieve delivered copies or undo accepted orders.

Evidence: preserve source attribution, verification status and linked corrections. Hashes establish integrity of committed content, not physical truth, complete history or the latest state. Exported proof packages enable offline checks; future service still needs an operator. Personal records need separate retention and redaction rules.
