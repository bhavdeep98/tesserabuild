Competitive landscape · Sept 2026

Digs validated the category. They did not close the identity gap.

Tessera is a property-bound living identity born from builder systems, carried through close, and persisted across owners — not a project portal, binder replacement, or public-records report.

One-line map: Digs owns build-time AI + handoff copy. Higharc owns design-to-build. Hyphen / Procore / Salesforce own builder ops. BuildFax / Verisk own insurance history from permits. Centriq / HomeZada / HomeBinder own owner-side binders. Nationals ship their own homeowner apps. None own the property identity that survives close and resale as one continuous record.

Primary rival: Digs

Aug 25, 2026 — Builders FirstSource solo-led Digs’ $25.3M Series A and signed a five-year commercial agreement to embed Digs across BFS’s digital ecosystem (~140k professional builders in reach).

What Digs sells

Builder-paid AI workspace + homeowner handoff

  • Starts from construction documents: plans, selections, takeoffs, 3D twin, AskDigs AI chat, warranty / DigsCare.
  • At handoff, Digs copies the binder into a new homeowner project; the builder keeps a separate project.
  • CEO language: “Carfax for the home.” Product is still a document portal, not a parcel-native identity.
  • No mortgage, title, or cross-system close risk. They do not sit across the buyer-chosen lender boundary.
Why Tessera still wins the identity

Same Carfax metaphor — different architecture. Digs forks the record at handoff. Tessera keeps one Home Passport through close and ownership change. Predictability (Kestrel) is the builder adoption driver that gets the verified data born; Identity + Ledger keep it trustworthy after the builder walks away.

Sources: BLDR IR (Aug 25, 2026) · digs.com · Digs support: homeowner handoff creates a distinct project

Nationals already have UX apps

Pulte, Lennar, and D.R. Horton ship homeowner experience applications. They are not waiting for “an app.” What they still lack — and what Digs does not supply — is a neutral, property-bound passport that spans close parties and survives warranty / resale, plus cross-system predictability on the path to close.

What they have

Branded portals for selections, warranties, service tickets, and post-close communication. Owned UX is table stakes for a national.

What they still need

A record that is not stranded in the builder’s login when warranty ends; intelligence that reads construction + mortgage + title together; a channel that can include lenders, title, and providers without becoming another ERP.

Adjacent field

Shut down — lesson

Centriq

Digital binder; claimed 400k+ homes. Shut down Jan 31, 2026; data deleted / migrated. Proof that binder apps without property-native, institutionally anchored persistence die — and take homeowner data with them.

HomeZada

Homeowner SaaS binder. Optional paid transfer of a published subset — not automatic parcel continuity. Starts at ownership, not build + close.

HomeBinder

B2B2C via inspectors / lenders / agents. Strong partner distribution; thin construction birth; not title/mortgage risk fabric.

BuildFax / Verisk

Permit-derived history for insurers. Public records about an address — not the builder’s installed home or warranties.

Higharc

Design → estimate → docs. $95M Series C (2026); >$170M total. Richest possible birth data for a passport — upstream partner, not ownership identity.

Hyphen / Procore / Salesforce

Builder systems of record. Tessera ingests them read-only. They stop at warranty / CRM; they do not become the property identity.

AsBuilt

Closest narrative twin: record built during construction, transferable binder messaging. Early / pilot; no close stack. Watch list.

Name collision: HomeFax.us, Hive HomeFax, Haushld and others already use “Carfax for homes.” Keep the product name Home Passport. Use Carfax as analogy only — Digs already owns that line in builder distribution.

Why now

  1. The close still leaks margin. Census / NAHB: of single-family homes built for sale and completed in 2025, 31.5% sold after completion (was 27.0% in 2024). More finished inventory sits waiting on coordination the builder ERP cannot see alone. (Eye on Housing)
  2. The category just got distribution — as a document portal. Digs + BFS are about to put a living home record in front of a huge share of builders. That record is being defined as a forked project binder. The identity thesis has to be obvious before that sticks.
  3. Handoff is still a binder. Pro Builder × Purtrack Post-Closing Handoff Benchmark (May–Jun 2026): 77% of responding builders still hand a physical binder. Sample n=22 — directional, matches what we see inside builders. (Pro Builder)
Bottom line for investors: Digs validates demand and language with capital and distribution. Tessera’s wedge is the missing product — property identity born in the build, authenticated through close, and surviving every subsequent owner — with predictability as the reason a builder connects us tomorrow.
