When the happy path breaks

Keep the rule. Change the recovery.

These are the same examples under stress—not a different architecture.

Handoff: someone else scans the plaque first.
The scan identifies the home but creates no ownership rights. Keep the claim pending approved evidence. Conflicting claims go to a reviewed dispute process; do not award ownership to the first scanner.
Internet: Maya revokes access after the provider has the details.
Stop future disclosures and prevent new jobs from dispatching. Request cancellation for already dispatched jobs and show whether it was confirmed. Previously delivered copies are governed by provider retention obligations; token revocation cannot retrieve them.
Internet: the provider times out or reports incorrect work.
For a timeout, query the job status using its stable identifier; retry only with safe provider idempotency. Otherwise reconcile manually. For an incorrect work report, preserve the source assertion and add a linked correction or dispute. Do not silently rewrite history or call every report verified.
Resale: an old-owner request arrives during closing.
Serialize authorization against the home’s ownership epoch. Requests authorized after transfer must use the new epoch. Old queued work is blocked; work already accepted outside Tessera must be reconciled. A distributed service cannot recall bytes already released.
Continuity: Tessera becomes unavailable.
New protected access fails closed during an outage; tested backups support recovery. Owners who already exported records can still read them. Independent proof packages can support offline verification of committed content. Future service and ownership changes still require an operating successor.

Deployment contract · start small without sharing authority

Recommended first deployment: a modular application for ownership, policy, home records and the audit transaction; separate connector workers and provider workers. Avoid a microservice per diagram box. Use a managed relational database, encrypted object storage, a queue and a secrets manager; vendor selection remains open.

Separate builder, home-history and personal-vault schemas with distinct database roles. Enforce tenant/home predicates with row-level security and non-owner application roles; use restricted database operations for cross-boundary projections. No client-selected tenant context is trusted. Schema separation alone is not a security boundary.

Keep provider operational metadata separate: store job IDs, statuses and payload digests, not raw PII. If delivery requires a payload snapshot, keep it encrypted in a restricted delivery store with an explicit expiry. Separate cloud audit logging and backups from application administration. Privileged infrastructure administrators remain part of the threat model.

Connector safety combines source-issued read scopes, endpoint allowlists, response validation and egress restrictions. HTTP verbs alone are insufficient; some read APIs use POST. Runtime upgrades and migration roles require reviewed changes. Authenticate provider callbacks, deduplicate events and quarantine untrusted result attachments.

Proof contract · reproducible exports, privacy and freshness

Define canonical serialization and algorithm versions. Use a fresh cryptographically random 256-bit nonce per committed event, and domain-separated leaf/node hashes. Commit event ID, version, predecessor and payload digest. Do not publish stable home identifiers or personal data. Fixed-cadence batches with an explicit padding policy reduce activity leakage; do not claim zero metadata leakage.

An authorized offline export includes exact committed bytes or clearly marked redacted representations, required nonces, evidence digests, inclusion paths, root receipts, signing-key history and verifier instructions. A redacted object will not hash like its original: verify a separately attested redacted projection or use a deliberately designed selective-disclosure scheme.

Root sequence numbers, independently monitored checkpoints and consistency proofs where applicable help detect conflicting or truncated log views. “Latest” is always relative to a trusted observed checkpoint; a standalone offline export cannot know about later events. Display publication time and pending/unanchored status.

Use portable formats and test verification without Tessera’s API. An export supports document continuity. Record hosting, new ownership verification and service coordination still need a successor operator; that business and operational arrangement is unresolved.

Technical reference: RFC 9162: Merkle inclusion and consistency proofs. Its transparency mechanisms are a reference, not evidence that Tessera has implemented them.

Operational acceptance · proposed targets, not measured results

Before a pilot, agree measurable availability, recovery and retention targets with the customer. Starting proposals: authorization p95 under 300 ms under agreed load; core read availability 99.9% monthly; backup recovery point within 15 minutes and recovery within 4 hours. These are design targets to cost and validate, not commitments made by this document.

Track unauthorized-access test outcomes, grant-revocation race tests, missing audit events, stale source data, ambiguous jobs, provider reconciliation time, transfer failures and export verification failures. Alert on anchor backlog independently from core service health. Run restore and credential-compromise exercises before widening the pilot.

Commercial evidence: measure handoff completion, time spent assembling records, successful activation rate and support effort against a pre-pilot baseline. For closing-risk claims, evaluate historical calibration and operational impact separately. No performance or revenue result is asserted here.

Ownership, grants and actions · shared invariants

Ownership: UNCLAIMED → CLAIM_PENDING → ACTIVE → TRANSFER_PENDING → ACTIVE (new epoch). A dispute freezes risky authority changes. Co-owners have independent verified bindings; the proposed initial voluntary-transfer policy requires all verified owners. Exceptional cases need an approved recovery route.

Transfer: one transaction locks the home authority row, increments the epoch, closes old bindings, creates new bindings and writes audit/outbox events. All old-epoch grants, including co-owner grants, cease to authorize new requests.

Grant: names issuer, recipient, home, epoch, purpose, fields, actions, expiry and applicable agreement version. Builder-originated sharing and owner-initiated service use distinct reviewed agreement routes; owner service must not depend on a surviving original-builder contract.

Order: DRAFT → APPROVED → QUEUED → DISPATCHING → CONFIRMED → COMPLETED, with explicit unknown, cancellation and manual-review states. Accepted terms and an idempotency key accompany the durable job. Revocation blocks new authorization, not already accepted external effects.

Audit: persist disclosure intent before releasing bytes; record dispatch and outcome separately. An audit write failure blocks a new release. A delayed external anchor does not block core service. Never imply every privileged infrastructure action is captured by application logging alone.

Where this leaves the team

Build and review in the same order.

01 / Prove the handoff

One builder. One buyer.

Agree the closing evidence policy and the exact fields that become home history.

Pass:

A stranger cannot claim the home; another tenant’s records cannot leak.

02 / Prove the service

One provider. One job.

Agree terms, data scope, safe retries, retention and cancellation behavior.

Pass:

Changed terms require approval; a timeout cannot silently duplicate an order.

03 / Prove the resale

Same home. New owner.

Agree co-owner exceptions, transfer rules and personal-record retention.

Pass:

Old grants fail; seller information stays private; the eligible history continues.

Key decisions: Product and closing partners approve ownership evidence. Data and privacy reviewers approve what transfers. Partnerships and engineering agree provider guarantees. Security approves enforcement tests. Founders decide whether independent witnessing and a continuity operator are required.
